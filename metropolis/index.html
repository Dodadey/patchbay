      PREVIEW nothing yet
      How the app reads these lines

      Type them here, or paste a column straight out of Numbers, Excel or a notes app. Tabs, commas, vertical bars and runs of spaces all work as separators, so you rarely have to think about it — and the preview shows exactly what the app made of each line before you commit anything.

      Leave the tempo out and the song simply gets no count-in. Leave beats-per-bar out and it assumes 4; leave count-in bars out and it assumes 1. Write the tempo however you like — 96, 96bpm and ♩=96 all read the same. Write the signature as 6/8, 3/4 or just 6; the top number is what gets counted. A line starting with # is ignored, so you can leave yourself notes.

      Don't know the timecodes yet? Put ? in the time column, or leave it out entirely. The songs get parked in the order you typed them, spread across the film and flagged as estimates — then play the film through, open CUES and hit STAMP NEXT as each one arrives to pin down the real timecode.

      LOAD CURRENT fills the box with the songs already in the sheet, in this same format, so you can edit the lot as text and put it back with REPLACE ALL SONGS. Scenes and act markers are never touched by either button.

      FILM WINDOW

      Pick your copy of the film and the app drives its own clock from the video — no calibration needed, scrubbing and pre-roll seek the picture with you. Nothing is uploaded; the file never leaves the iPad. Leave it empty when the film comes from a projector or another machine and the app will free-run instead, which is what the sync points are for. iPadOS forgets the file on reload, so re-pick it after a restart.

      Pick file is the clock when this iPad's copy is the only film in the room — rehearsal, or the picture coming off the iPad itself. Nothing can drift, and scrubbing seeks the picture. Pick file follows when the audience is watching a projector or another machine: that is a second, independent player, so the app free-runs, you hold it to the projected print with the sync points, and this copy gets dragged along behind as a reference picture rather than being believed.
      Seconds to add to the video's own time to get film time. Use it if your file carries a leader, a logo, or a black head before frame one of the picture.
